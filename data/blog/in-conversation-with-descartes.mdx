##  In Conversation With Descartes

Interviewer: Mr Descartes, not the des cartes by which we play but the Descartes which thinks and therefore exists is joining us in this philosophic day. Mr Descartes you’re amongst the first influential figures in the modern western philosophical tradition. Mr Descartes, you’re known for your doubt concerning everything, what motivated you for such a pursuit?

Descartes: it is a very well affirmed maxim that the path to certainty is begot in doubt. The person that isn’t certain from doubt is certain from arrogance. For only doubt is the beginning of any intellectual pursuit. As much as an argument cannot be concluded without a first premise, certainty cannot be concluded without a first doubt.

The truth is begot by doubt, it is a noteworthy insight. And along such a path, you have doubted and denied all your beliefs and knowledge. Was everything then denied?

Descartes: I doubted all, and in doubting everything I assured nothing, except for the fact that I doubt, the fact that I am an intellect capable of doubt.

Some say that you have doubted everything but forgot to doubt the fact that you doubt. What is your response to these people?

Descartes: I did doubt the fact that I doubt. Just as I mentioned, the fact that I doubt assures that I possess the faculty of doubt. For having denied all of physical matter, all of history and every other conceived notion of the world, I could not deny my denial and doubt. I could not deny or doubt the fact that I think. For the faculty of doubt even if exercised upon it-self is its very proof.

Having doubted everything, how could you arrive at certainty of the things you doubted? After all, it all could be but a dream as you have said, or the working of our minds by some demon deceived.

Descartes: Indeed, the light of things would remain in eternal doubt had it not been for god.

Paron me, but I do not understand

Descartes: Remained solely with the fact that I am a finite mental substance, it was through god that I could be certain of anything, for god is not a deceiver.

So how do you prove god?

Descartes: The very fact that I am a finite mental substance yet with the knowledge of an infinite mental substance proves the latter’s existence, namely god.

My apology Mr Descartes, but the argument is somewhat difficult to grasp.

Descartes: My mind is finite and all its knowledge is derived from experience which is also finite. But how could what‘s finite conceive of what’s infinite. That is to say, how could my finite mind deriving its ideas from the world that is both finite and imperfect, possess an idea of a thing that is both infinite and perfect.

Sounds impossible. So how could man conceive of the idea of god?

Descartes: Precisely that, there is but one single unavoidable conclusion.

That the idea is innate in my mind, that god in having created my mental substance have inserted the idea of the infinite in order to know of god.

Fascinating. in doubting everything you are certain of your intellect, and in assuring your intellect you discover within it the idea of the infinite, and through the idea of the infinite you are certain of god, and through god therefrom you are certain of the world.

Descartes: Precisely so

In rediscovering that your body does exist, why did you separate mind from it?

Descartes: What is denied at first is separated at second. Being primarily certain only of a thinking soul, the thinking soul does not reside within space, your ideas and mine possess no physicality, they carry no weight and as such they are separated from the body.

Would I be correct to conclude the soul is primary and the body is secondary?

Desartes: Yes, quite so.

Doubt is a feeling, do feelings too reside within the soul?

Descartes: Of course

So only humans are capable of feeling pain? Couldn’t animals feel any pain?

Descartes: Only humans possess a soul, unlike of what Aristotle conceived, animals have no soul, the proof of that is that animals possess no degree of rationality, they possess not a single thought.

So when animals shriek of pain, what is the explanation of it?

Descartes: It is an automated response, and nothing else, the voice after all is a bodily function.

We thank you for your collaboration

Descartes: My pleasure

Now we are to take the opinions of some other guests concerning the thought of today. First we have with us mr gilbert ryle. Mr gilbert ryle what is your opinion of what has been said?

Ryles: Mr Descartes dualism, of body and mind is a categorical fallacy. Just as materialists and idealists are reductionist in describing existence as existing only by the mind or only by matter, Mr Descartes in thinking mind intelligent and matter unintelligent, falsely concluded that they must be separate in nature in their entirety.

Mr Locke

Locke: I object to the proof by infinity, for not a single idea is innate in our minds from birth. The concept of infinity is simply derived from experience by means of addition; infinity is nothing else but eternal addition, a constant imagined forth motion.

and finally to conclude, Mr george berkley which is unfortunately not here with us, has nonetheless contacted us, and spoke to us bits of his formulated philosophy in response to the cartesian philosophy. in which he objects most strongly to the separate coexistence of cartesian duality. if body and soul be different in nature berekley wrote to us, then how could the sensations of pain or pleasure in the body, be felt immediately and most strongly in the soul? it is nothing except for an impossibility.
